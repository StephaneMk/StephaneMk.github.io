The method

Four verbs, from need to use

Every assignment follows the same loop: it starts with a business question and ends in a team’s hands.

  1. 01

    Understand

    Start from a team’s question: its management process, its products, its transactions, its constraints.

  2. 02

    Find

    Identify the data that answers it: which database, which table, which field.

  3. 03

    Build

    Collect, clean and turn the data into a readable indicator.

  4. 04

    Share

    Put the indicator in the teams’ hands: dashboard, alert, business view, training.

The emblem

A monogram and a dot

My initials in Fraunces italic, followed by a pink dot: the same dot that ends “Clarify.” on my portfolio. It signals a clear conclusion.

  • Minimum size 24 px per side on screen.
  • Clear space a quarter of the emblem’s width all around.
  • Fixed shape a rounded square, never stretched or rotated.

Visual signature

The diagram, more than the logo

My work is recognisable by the way I draw a flow: from the business need to the indicator in use. The same grammar everywhere: README header, posts, presentations and articles.

  • One diagram per case study and one per technical article.
  • Named steps in the words of the business, not of the tool.
  • A single result highlighted, at the end of the flow.

Rules

What does not change

The rules that keep the identity consistent, page after page and profile after profile.

Always

  • A single accent: pink, deep on light backgrounds and bright on dark ones.
  • Dark top of page, cream content, dark footer.
  • Headings in Fraunces, text and interface in Geist.
  • Verifiable facts: named tools, real figures, exact role (“set up” or “took part”).
  • Address visitors as “you”, use “I” for my own work, sentence case throughout.
  • AA-compliant contrast for all text.
  • One key figure highlighted per block, the others in support.
  • Short texts without a full stop: captions, notes, labels.

Never

  • Blue, cyan or decorative purple gradients.
  • An isolated coloured section in the middle of a light page.
  • A hover effect on an element that is not clickable.
  • Internal names of my employer’s projects, teams or applications.
  • Rounded or invented figures, or exclamation marks.
  • A distorted emblem, recoloured outside the palette or placed on a busy background.
  • Spaced capitals or decorative numbering (“01 /”) in eyebrows.
  • Pill-shaped buttons or effects that lift or distort an element on hover.
